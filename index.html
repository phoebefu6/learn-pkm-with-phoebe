Learn PKM with Phoebe

Count what is in the place you keep things. Then count the last five times you needed something you knew you had saved, and how many you actually got back. Those two numbers are much further apart than anyone expects, and closing the gap is not a tidiness problem. Six sessions on the loop that makes a collection pay: what to capture and what to call it, where a note lives, what makes it reachable eight months later, and a review short enough to survive a bad week. Tool-agnostic, and honest about what the evidence does and does not support.

The six sessions 🎯 · one collection, six passes

The running situation is a real-shaped collection at Daybreak, the coffee-subscription company from the sibling courses: eighteen months of one person's notes, forty-eight of them, two live projects, a reference shelf and six notes called things like "Interesting". Every session does something different to that same collection, and session 4 asks it sixteen questions and counts how many it can still answer.

What is honest here 🧾

There is no controlled study showing that PARA, a second brain, or any packaged note method improves what you retrieve or produce. That absence is the starting point of session 1, not something worked around. What is evidenced is the mechanisms these methods borrow, and those are quoted with both halves of every finding.

ClaimStatus
Found by looking, found by searching, lost; reachability, orphans, links, tag spreadReally computed from the collection's own graph: a real traversal, a real folder walk, a real keyword match
The collection itselfConstructed. Forty-eight notes written to be realistic. No published figures on real collection size, orphan rate or link density were found, so the numbers describe this collection and nothing else
The verdict line, and the thresholdsJudgement, badged as a heuristic and printed on the widget so they can be argued with. The ordering of the five filings does not depend on them
"PARA is proven to work"Not claimed. Searched for directly across several angles; what exists is a separate, survey-based, correlational literature that does not test these methods
Retrieval practice and spacingQuoted with real numbers from the papers, including the ones the internet gets wrong: no "spacing doubles recall" figure exists in the paper it is attributed to
"Most people never reread their notes"Not stated. No study was found; the figure in circulation traces to one blogger testing their own workflow
TaggingMeasured as cost and return in this collection only. No claim that tagging harms retrieval; the literature reports no clean winner between folders and tags
